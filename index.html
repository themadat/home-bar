          Ingredient substitutions

          Aperol and Campari

          Aperol = sweet orange with gentle bitterness.
          Campari = bitter orange with a much stronger herbal bite.

          They can substitute for each other, but they’ll substantially change the drink. Campari in an Aperol Spritz makes it darker, stronger, and much more bitter. Aperol in a Negroni makes it sweeter, lighter, and less assertive.

          Cointreau, triple sec, and Grand Marnier

          Triple sec is a category. Cointreau is a premium, 40% ABV triple sec. Grand Marnier blends orange liqueur with Cognac for oak, vanilla, and richness.

          Cointreau ↔ triple sec: substitute 1:1. A lower-proof triple sec may taste sweeter and less intensely orange; reduce other simple or agave syrup to taste.

          Cointreau → Grand Marnier: substitute 1:1 for a richer, heavier, less crisp drink. Grand Marnier → Cointreau: substitute 1:1 for a brighter, cleaner drink without Cognac richness.

          Chartreuse colors

          Green (Verte) = powerful, spicy, intensely herbal.
          Yellow (Jaune) = sweeter, softer, honeyed and floral.

          They aren’t usually a 1:1 swap: Green can overpower a Yellow recipe, while Yellow makes a Green recipe sweeter and much less herbal.

          Yellow substitutes: Strega is a mint-and-saffron, honeyed 1:1 option; Dolin Génépy is earthier and works 1:1 in lighter drinks; Bénédictine is sweeter and spicier, so use slightly less or add citrus; Faccia Brutto Centerbe Giallo supplies a similarly assertive herbal profile.           Bénédictine D.O.M. and B&B

          For most cocktails, start with a 1:1 substitution. B&B makes the drink less sweet and less herbal, with more brandy character. That works particularly well in whiskey- and cognac-based drinks where brandy isn’t out of place.

          To make B&B behave more like D.O.M.: B&B is approximately 60% Bénédictine, so the mathematical equivalent is 1 oz Bénédictine ≈ 1⅔ oz B&B. That is usually a poor cocktail substitution because it also adds considerably more brandy and alcohol.

          A better practical replacement is 1 part Bénédictine → 1 part B&B plus a small amount of rich simple or honey syrup. For ½ oz Bénédictine, try ½ oz B&B + 1 tsp honey syrup to restore some lost richness.
